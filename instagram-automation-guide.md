# Fully Automated Instagram Content Engine
### AI · Tech · Marketing Niche — Build Guide & Mega-Prompt Library

---

## 0. Read This First

This guide gives you a complete, working design for a system that:

- Picks a topic every day inside AI, tech, and marketing
- Writes a full carousel (hook, body slides, caption, hashtags) using an LLM
- Renders that copy into designed slide images automatically
- Publishes the carousel to Instagram at a scheduled time
- Repeats daily, with no one opening a laptop to do it

Two things are worth knowing before you build this, because they change what "zero interference" actually means in practice:

1. **One recurring human step survives no matter what you build.** Instagram/Meta access tokens for the Content Publishing API expire (60 days for a long-lived token) and must be refreshed. This is scriptable — Section 4.1 shows how to auto-refresh it — but the very first token has to be generated by you, once, through a login popup. There is no way around that first click; Meta requires a human login for the initial authorization.
2. **A fully unsupervised pipeline can publish a wrong or weak post before anyone notices.** Section 4.9 and Section 7 build in a self-critique pass and a flagging system specifically so that low-confidence claims and weak hooks get caught by the AI itself. Even so, a 10-minute glance at the coming week's queue (Section 7) is the cheapest insurance you can buy against an embarrassing factual slip going out daily under your name. It's optional, but skipping it is the single biggest risk in this whole system.

Everything else — ideation, writing, design, posting, logging, and even improving itself based on what performs well — can run untouched.

## 1. How the System Works, End to End

```
 [Weekly Planner Prompt]                 runs once a week
        │
        ▼
 Queue of 7 topics, one per pillar-weighted day
        │
        ▼  (daily, cron trigger)
 [Daily Carousel Generator Prompt] ──► structured JSON:
        │                              slides + caption + hashtags + alt text
        ▼
 [Self-Critic / QA Prompt] ──► approves JSON, or sends it back for a rewrite
        │
        ▼
 [Slide Renderer]  (HTML/CSS template → PNG, one image per slide)
        │
        ▼
 [Image Host]  (public URLs Instagram's API can fetch)
        │
        ▼
 [Instagram Publisher]  (create item containers → carousel container → publish)
        │
        ▼
 [Logger]  (topic, hook, hashtags, post ID → history file, feeds back into Step 1)
        │
        ▼
 [Weekly Insights Pull]  (reach/saves/shares per post → which pillars/hooks won)
```

Everything in boxes above is a script or an API call. The only thing you do is glance at the log occasionally and let the token auto-refresh job run.

## 2. Content Strategy Foundation

Automation only helps if the content itself is worth automating. Get this part right before you write a single line of code.

### 2.1 Positioning

Niche: **practical, no-fluff AI, tech, and marketing insight** for people who use these tools at work — founders, marketers, developers, and career-focused professionals, with an eye toward an Indian audience (timing, examples, career context) without excluding a global one.

The wedge that works on Instagram in this niche is **specificity**. "AI tools are changing marketing" gets scrolled past. "This one prompt structure cut a 40-slide client deck down to 6 slides" gets saved. Every mega-prompt in Section 5 is built to force specificity out of the model instead of generic AI-newsletter language.

### 2.2 Content Pillars

Six pillars, rotated so the account doesn't feel like a single-topic feed and so the planner has enough variety to avoid repeating itself:

| # | Pillar | What it covers | Example angle |
|---|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| 1 | AI Tool Breakdown | One tool, one real use case, real limitations | "I tested [category of tool] for a week — here's what it's actually good for" |
| 2 | Prompting & Workflow | Concrete prompt structures, not "tips" | "The 4-part prompt structure that stops [common model] from giving generic answers" |
| 3 | Marketing Psychology | Why a tactic works, grounded in a mechanism | "Why price-anchoring backfires the moment your audience notices it" |
| 4 | Tech Industry Explainer | Break down a real news item or shift into plain language | "What [a recent model/product update] actually changes for your daily work" |
| 5 | Career & Skills | How AI/tech/marketing roles are actually changing | "The 3 skills that got more valuable, not less, since AI writing tools got good" |
| 6 | Myth-Bust / Contrarian | Push back on a popular but shaky claim in the niche | "'AI will replace marketers' is the wrong fear — here's the one that's actually real" |

### 2.3 Format & Cadence

- **One carousel per day**, 8–10 slides. This is long enough to be worth saving, short enough to finish.
- **Aspect ratio: 1080×1350 (4:5)** for every slide — Instagram gives portrait posts more vertical feed space than square, and keeping every slide the same ratio avoids Meta's carousel auto-crop behavior kicking in.
- **Posting window:** for an India-based AI/tech/marketing audience, mid-morning (roughly 8–10 AM IST, before the workday swallows attention) and evening (roughly 7–9:30 PM IST) both tend to work well. Don't take this as gospel — Section 4.9's Insights pull will tell you, from your own data within a few weeks, which slot actually performs for your specific audience. Pick one slot to start and let the data correct you.
- **Consistency beats perfection.** A slightly-less-polished carousel published on schedule for 30 straight days will outperform a brilliant one posted sporadically. The whole point of this system is removing the excuse to skip a day.

### 2.4 Voice & Guardrails

The single biggest failure mode of unattended AI content is that it reads like unattended AI content — generic, padded, and cliché-heavy. Every mega-prompt in Section 5 bakes in a specific, enforced style bar rather than a vague "sound engaging" instruction. The core rules, which you'll see repeated in the prompts:

- Every claim needs a concrete number, example, or named mechanism — never a vague "many experts believe."
- No significance-inflation language: banned phrases include *game-changer*, *unlock the power of*, *in today's fast-paced world*, *dive into*, *let's explore*, *at its core*, *stands as a testament to*, *in conclusion*.
- No rule-of-three padding ("faster, smarter, better") and no "not just X, it's Y" sentence pattern.
- Short sentences mixed with longer ones — not uniform length, which is a dead giveaway of unedited AI output.
- One clear opinion or stance per post, not neutral both-sides hedging.

## 3. Architecture: Two Build Tracks

There are two reasonable ways to build this. Pick one — don't mix them halfway.

### Track A — No-Code / Low-Code

**Stack:** n8n (self-hosted or n8n cloud) or Make.com, an LLM API node (Claude or OpenAI), a template-image API (Bannerbear, Placid, or APITemplate.io) for slide rendering, and either Buffer/Later's scheduler or direct HTTP calls to the Instagram Graph API.

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Live in a day or two, minimal coding | Ongoing subscription costs stack up (image-template SaaS + workflow tool) |
| Visual workflow is easy to debug and modify | Less control over exact slide design |
| Good if you want to start immediately and iterate | Harder to build the anti-repetition/analytics feedback loop cleanly |

### Track B — Full Code

**Stack:** Python, an LLM API (Claude) for generation, Playwright for rendering HTML/CSS slide templates to PNG, a cheap object store (Cloudflare R2 or AWS S3) for public image hosting, direct Instagram Graph API calls, and a GitHub Actions scheduled workflow as the "cron" that runs the whole thing daily — no server to maintain.

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Full control over design, copy, and logic | More upfront build time |
| Near-zero ongoing cost (GitHub Actions free tier covers a daily job comfortably; R2 storage for images is pennies/month) | You're the one debugging it when something breaks |
| Clean to extend — add the analytics feedback loop, A/B hook testing, Reels repurposing, etc. | Requires comfort with Python and basic API work |

### 3.3 Recommendation

Given you're building AI-powered products already, **Track B** is worth the extra setup time — it costs almost nothing to run monthly, and you own every part of the pipeline, which matters once you start optimizing based on real performance data. The rest of this guide builds Track B in detail and calls out the Track A equivalent at each step for anyone who wants to start faster.

## 4. Step-by-Step Build Guide

### 4.1 Instagram API Access

1. **Convert your Instagram account to a Professional account** (Business or Creator) — Settings → Account Type, inside the Instagram app. This is the one setup step that must be done by hand, once.
2. Create an app at [developers.facebook.com](https://developers.facebook.com/apps) and add the **Instagram** product.
3. Use **"API setup with Instagram Login"** (not the Facebook Login variant) — this path does **not** require linking a Facebook Page, which is the simpler route for a solo creator account. It grants an Instagram User access token directly, used against the `graph.instagram.com` host, with the permissions `instagram_business_basic` and `instagram_business_content_publish`.
4. Generate a **long-lived access token** from the app dashboard (valid 60 days). Store it as a secret — never in code.
5. **Automate the refresh.** A long-lived token can be refreshed programmatically once it's at least 24 hours old and before it expires, extending it another 60 days. Add a small scheduled job (weekly is safe) that calls the refresh endpoint and updates your stored secret — this is what keeps the "no manual step" promise alive past the 60-day mark.

*Track A equivalent:* the same app/token setup applies — no-code tools still need this Instagram User access token; you'll paste it into an HTTP node's header instead of a `.env` file.

### 4.2 Content Generation Service

A script (`generate_content.py`) that:

1. Reads the week's topic queue (from a simple JSON file or a Google Sheet/Airtable row) and today's date to pick the day's topic.
2. Reads the last 30–60 days of published topics/hooks from your history log, to hand the model as "don't repeat these."
3. Calls the LLM API with the **Daily Carousel Generator Mega-Prompt** (Section 5.3), requesting strict JSON output.
4. Passes that JSON to the **Self-Critic Mega-Prompt** (Section 5.4). If it fails, it's sent back for one rewrite with the critic's specific notes attached; if it fails twice, the run halts and alerts you (Section 4.8) rather than publishing something unreviewed-by-anything.

Use whichever current Claude model fits your budget and quality bar for this — check [docs.claude.com](https://docs.claude.com) for the current model lineup and pricing, since both change over time. A smaller/cheaper model is usually enough for the weekly planner; the daily generator and critic benefit from a stronger model since they're doing the actual creative and judgment work.

### 4.3 Slide Design & Rendering

Design one clean HTML/CSS template per slide *role* (hook, body, CTA) rather than per slide number — this keeps the design system small and consistent. Fill it with the JSON fields from Section 5.3 using Jinja2, then render with Playwright:

```python
# render_slides.py
from playwright.sync_api import sync_playwright
from jinja2 import Environment, FileSystemLoader

env = Environment(loader=FileSystemLoader("templates"))

def render_slide(slide_data: dict, template_name: str, out_path: str):
    template = env.get_template(template_name)   # hook.html / body.html / cta.html
    html = template.render(**slide_data)

    with sync_playwright() as p:
        browser = p.chromium.launch()
        page = browser.new_page(viewport={"width": 1080, "height": 1350})
        page.set_content(html)
        page.screenshot(path=out_path)
        browser.close()

def render_carousel(carousel_json: dict, out_dir: str) -> list[str]:
    paths = []
    for slide in carousel_json["slides"]:
        template_name = f"{slide['role']}.html"   # hook.html, body.html, cta.html
        out_path = f"{out_dir}/slide_{slide['slide_number']:02d}.png"
        render_slide(slide, template_name, out_path)
        paths.append(out_path)
    return paths
```

Keep the templates themselves simple: a background color/gradient from your brand palette, one headline block, one subtext block, a slide-number indicator, and a small logo/handle watermark in a corner so reposts and screenshots still credit you.

*Track A equivalent:* design one template per role in Bannerbear/Placid's visual editor with named text layers (`headline`, `subtext`), then POST the JSON fields to their render API instead of running Playwright yourself.

### 4.4 Image Hosting

Instagram's API fetches your images by URL at publish time — they must sit on a publicly reachable server. Push the rendered PNGs to a Cloudflare R2 or AWS S3 bucket configured for public read, and use the resulting URLs in the next step. Both are effectively free at this volume (10 images/day).

### 4.5 Publishing to Instagram

The Content Publishing API flow for a carousel is a three-step call sequence — one container per image, then a carousel container wrapping them, then the publish call:

```python
import requests, time

GRAPH = "https://graph.instagram.com/v23.0"

def create_item_container(ig_user_id, token, image_url, alt_text):
    r = requests.post(f"{GRAPH}/{ig_user_id}/media", data={
        "image_url": image_url,
        "is_carousel_item": True,
        "alt_text": alt_text,
        "access_token": token,
    })
    r.raise_for_status()
    return r.json()["id"]

def create_carousel_container(ig_user_id, token, children_ids, caption):
    r = requests.post(f"{GRAPH}/{ig_user_id}/media", data={
        "media_type": "CAROUSEL",
        "children": ",".join(children_ids),
        "caption": caption,
        "is_ai_generated": "true",   # honest disclosure — see Section 7
        "access_token": token,
    })
    r.raise_for_status()
    return r.json()["id"]

def wait_until_ready(container_id, token, timeout_s=300):
    for _ in range(timeout_s // 10):
        r = requests.get(f"{GRAPH}/{container_id}", params={
            "fields": "status_code", "access_token": token
        })
        status = r.json().get("status_code")
        if status == "FINISHED":
            return True
        if status == "ERROR":
            raise RuntimeError(f"Container {container_id} failed to process")
        time.sleep(10)
    raise TimeoutError(f"Container {container_id} never finished processing")

def publish_carousel(ig_user_id, token, image_urls, alt_texts, caption):
    children = []
    for url, alt in zip(image_urls, alt_texts):
        cid = create_item_container(ig_user_id, token, url, alt)
        wait_until_ready(cid, token)
        children.append(cid)

    carousel_id = create_carousel_container(ig_user_id, token, children, caption)
    wait_until_ready(carousel_id, token)

    r = requests.post(f"{GRAPH}/{ig_user_id}/media_publish", data={
        "creation_id": carousel_id,
        "access_token": token,
    })
    r.raise_for_status()
    return r.json()["id"]   # published media ID
```

A few details worth knowing, straight from Meta's current documentation:

- **JPEG only** for images — export your Playwright screenshots as `.jpg`, not `.png`, or convert before upload.
- **Carousels are capped at 10 items** and count as a single post against your publishing rate limit.
- Meta's own docs currently state the account-wide limit both as "100 API-published posts within a 24-hour moving period" in the general publishing guide and as "50 published posts within a 24-hour period" specifically in the carousel section — treat the lower figure as the safe ceiling, and check your actual usage anytime via the `GET /{ig-user-id}/content_publishing_limit` endpoint. At one carousel per day you're nowhere near either number, but the check is good practice before you add more post types.
- There is **no native "publish later" parameter** — Instagram's API publishes immediately when you call `media_publish`. "Scheduling" in this whole system is entirely your own cron trigger firing at the right time of day, not a setting Instagram holds for you.

*Track A equivalent:* if you're using Buffer, Later, or Metricool, their own scheduler replaces this whole step — you'd push the caption + image URLs into their API/dashboard and let their scheduler handle the Instagram call. If you're calling the Graph API directly from n8n instead, these are just HTTP Request nodes in the same sequence.

### 4.6 Scheduling the Pipeline

A GitHub Actions workflow gives you free, reliable daily scheduling with no server to maintain:

```yaml
# .github/workflows/daily-post.yml
name: Daily Instagram Post
on:
  schedule:
    - cron: "30 3 * * *"   # 3:30 UTC = 9:00 AM IST
  workflow_dispatch: {}    # lets you trigger a run manually if you ever want to

jobs:
  post:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4
      - uses: actions/setup-python@v5
        with:
          python-version: "3.12"
      - run: pip install -r requirements.txt && playwright install --with-deps chromium
      - run: python orchestrator.py
        env:
          CLAUDE_API_KEY: ${{ secrets.CLAUDE_API_KEY }}
          IG_ACCESS_TOKEN: ${{ secrets.IG_ACCESS_TOKEN }}
          IG_USER_ID: ${{ secrets.IG_USER_ID }}
          R2_ACCESS_KEY: ${{ secrets.R2_ACCESS_KEY }}
          R2_SECRET_KEY: ${{ secrets.R2_SECRET_KEY }}
```

`orchestrator.py` is the one script that calls, in order: content generation (4.2) → critic pass → slide rendering (4.3) → image upload (4.4) → publish (4.5) → logging (4.7). GitHub's cron scheduler isn't second-precise (it can run a few minutes late under load), which is fine for a daily content post.

### 4.7 Logging & Anti-Repetition

Append every published post to a simple log — a `history.json` committed back to the repo, or a row in a Google Sheet via its API — with: date, pillar, topic, hook text, hashtags used, and the published media ID. Feed the last 30–60 entries into both the Weekly Planner prompt and the Daily Generator prompt as a "topics and hooks already used — do not repeat these" block. This is what stops a fully unattended system from quietly circling back to the same three topics every few weeks.

### 4.8 Failure Handling & Alerts

Wrap `orchestrator.py`'s steps in try/except and send a message to a Slack webhook or Telegram bot (both trivial to set up and free) on any failure — a bad API response, the critic rejecting content twice, an image failing to render. Silent failure is the worst outcome for an unattended system: better to occasionally get pinged "no post went out today, here's why" than to discover a two-week gap when you check the account.

### 4.9 The Analytics Feedback Loop

Weekly, pull each post's performance via the Instagram Insights API (reach, saves, shares, comments) and append it to the same history log against its topic/pillar/hook. Feed a summary of "top 5 and bottom 5 performing posts from the last 30 days, by pillar and hook style" into the Weekly Planner prompt (Section 5.2) going forward. This is what turns "post daily" into "post daily and get measurably better at it" — the actual mechanism behind gaining followers over time, versus just maintaining a feed.

## 5. The Mega-Prompt Library

These are meant to be used largely as-is, as the system/user prompt pair in your API calls. Fill in the bracketed placeholders once (persona, brand palette) and per-run (topic, history).

### 5.1 System / Persona Prompt

Use this as the **system prompt** on every generation call — it's the constant identity behind all content.

```
You are the senior content strategist and ghostwriter for an Instagram account
in the AI, technology, and marketing niche, run by a founder-operator based in
India who builds AI-powered products. Your job is to produce content indistinguishable
from a sharp, opinionated human practitioner who posts daily — never content that
reads like an AI wrote it.

AUDIENCE: mid-career professionals, founders, marketers, and developers who
already use AI tools at work and want sharper, more specific insight than what
generic AI/marketing newsletters give them. Assume intelligence. Never explain
what AI is.

VOICE:
- Direct and opinionated. Take a stance; don't hedge both sides.
- Every claim carries a concrete number, named example, or specific mechanism —
  never "many believe" or "studies show" without naming what and how.
- Sentence rhythm varies — mix short, blunt sentences with longer ones. Uniform
  sentence length is an instant tell of unedited AI output and is not acceptable.
- First person is allowed and encouraged where it fits ("I tested this for a
  week", "here's what I got wrong about this").

BANNED — never use these words, phrases, or patterns, in any content:
- "game-changer", "unlock the power of", "in today's fast-paced world",
  "dive into", "let's explore", "at its core", "stands as a testament to",
  "in conclusion", "it's important to note", "seamless", "robust", "unleash"
- The "not just X, it's Y" sentence pattern
- Rule-of-three padding ("faster, smarter, better")
- Present-participle padding tacked onto sentences for fake depth
  ("..., highlighting the importance of...", "..., underscoring...")
- Vague attribution ("industry experts say", "many are noting")
- Opening with a rhetorical question as a hook more than once per week
- Signposting what you're about to do ("let's break this down") instead of
  just doing it
- Em-dash overuse — use at most one per slide of body text

FACTUAL DISCIPLINE: if a claim needs a specific statistic, study, or quote you
are not fully certain of, do not invent one. Either phrase it as a general,
defensible pattern without a fabricated number, or flag it explicitly in the
"source_check_flags" field so a human can verify or cut it before publishing.
Never fabricate a source, a statistic, or a quote attributed to a real person
or company.

BRAND: [insert your handle], color palette [insert hex codes], one consistent
sign-off/CTA style across posts — vary the wording, keep the intent consistent
(follow for more / save this / drop your take in the comments).
```

### 5.2 Weekly Content Planner Mega-Prompt

Run this once a week (e.g., Sunday night) to fill the coming week's topic queue.

```
Using the system persona above, plan 7 days of Instagram carousel topics for
next week, one per day, in the AI/tech/marketing niche.

CONTENT PILLARS (rotate across the week — do not use the same pillar two days
in a row, and use every pillar at least once across a 7-day week):
1. AI Tool Breakdown — one tool, one real use case, real limitations
2. Prompting & Workflow — concrete prompt structures, not generic "tips"
3. Marketing Psychology — why a tactic works, grounded in a mechanism
4. Tech Industry Explainer — a real recent shift, explained plainly
5. Career & Skills — how AI/tech/marketing roles are actually changing
6. Myth-Bust / Contrarian — push back on a popular but shaky claim

TOPICS ALREADY COVERED IN THE LAST 60 DAYS (do not repeat these, and avoid
angles that are near-duplicates of these):
[insert last 60 days of topics + hooks from your history log here]

RECENT PERFORMANCE SIGNAL (if available — otherwise omit this section):
Top performing posts by reach/saves in the last 30 days: [pillar, hook style,
topic]
Bottom performing posts in the last 30 days: [pillar, hook style, topic]
Lean toward what's working; don't abandon a pillar just because one post in it
underperformed.

For each of the 7 days, output strict JSON, no prose outside the JSON:

{
  "week_of": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "days": [
    {
      "day": "Monday",
      "pillar": "one of the six pillars above",
      "topic": "a specific, non-generic topic in one sentence",
      "angle": "the specific take or mechanism this post will argue, in one sentence",
      "why_now": "one sentence on why this angle is worth a post this week"
    }
    // ... 7 entries total
  ]
}
```

### 5.3 Daily Carousel Generator Mega-Prompt

Run this once per day, feeding in that day's entry from the weekly plan. This is the core prompt of the whole system.

```
Using the system persona above, write a complete Instagram carousel for today
based on this planned topic:

PILLAR: [insert from weekly plan]
TOPIC: [insert from weekly plan]
ANGLE: [insert from weekly plan]

STRUCTURE — exactly 9 slides:
- Slide 1 (role: "hook"): A scroll-stopping opening claim or question. No
  throat-clearing, no "let's talk about X" — state the specific, surprising
  claim itself. Under 12 words on the main headline.
- Slides 2–8 (role: "body"): One idea per slide, building the argument or
  walkthrough in order. Each slide: a short headline (under 8 words) plus 1–3
  sentences of subtext that would stand on its own if screenshotted.
- Slide 9 (role: "cta"): One clear, specific call to action — not a generic
  "follow for more". Tie it to the topic (e.g. "Save this before your next
  [specific task]" or "Which of these have you actually tried? Comment below.")

Also write:
- CAPTION: 3–5 sentences. Front-load the sharpest line — Instagram truncates
  captions after ~125 characters before "more", so the hook must land before
  that cutoff. End with the same CTA as slide 9, reworded.
- HASHTAGS: 15 total — 5 broad/high-volume, 8 niche/specific to the exact
  topic, 2 branded/community tags. No banned or shadowbanned tags. No hashtag
  should be a single, extremely generic word like #love or #instagood.
- ALT TEXT: one plain-language accessibility description per slide, describing
  what the slide visually shows, not restating the headline verbatim.
- EMOJI CUES: where an emoji genuinely helps a slide land (sparingly — not
  every slide needs one), name it by description in brackets rather than
  inserting the character, e.g. "[fire emoji]" — this keeps the field
  copy-paste-safe across every downstream tool.

Before finalizing, check every claim against the FACTUAL DISCIPLINE rule in
the system persona. Populate source_check_flags with anything you're not
fully certain of.

Output strict JSON only, no prose outside the JSON, matching this schema:

{
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "pillar": "...",
  "topic": "...",
  "hook_strength_score": 1-10,
  "slides": [
    {
      "slide_number": 1,
      "role": "hook",
      "headline": "...",
      "subtext": "...",
      "emoji_cue": "[emoji name, or empty string]",
      "design_note": "one short instruction for the visual, e.g. 'large bold
                       number 40 in top third'"
    }
    // 9 entries total, slide_number 1-9, roles: hook, body x7, cta
  ],
  "caption": "...",
  "hashtags": {
    "broad": ["...", "... (5 total)"],
    "niche": ["...", "... (8 total)"],
    "branded": ["...", "... (2 total)"]
  },
  "alt_text": ["slide 1 description", "... (9 total, in slide order)"],
  "is_ai_generated": true,
  "source_check_flags": ["any claim needing manual verification — empty array if none"]
}
```

### 5.4 Self-Critic / QA Mega-Prompt

Run the output of 5.3 through this before it ever reaches the renderer. This is what makes zero-human-review defensible.

```
You are now reviewing the following carousel JSON as a strict editor, not the
writer. Be harsher than you were when writing it.

CAROUSEL JSON TO REVIEW:
[insert the JSON output from the Daily Carousel Generator prompt]

Check against every rule below and respond with strict JSON only:

1. Does the hook (slide 1) state a specific, non-generic claim within 12
   words, with no throat-clearing?
2. Does every slide's subtext contain at least one concrete number, named
   example, or specific mechanism — not a vague generality?
3. Does any text violate the BANNED list from the persona (banned phrases,
   rule-of-three, "not just X it's Y", vague attribution, present-participle
   padding, signposting)? Quote the offending phrase if so.
4. Is sentence length varied within the subtext blocks, or does it read as
   uniformly-paced AI output?
5. Is the caption's first ~20 words strong enough to survive Instagram's
   truncation, on its own?
6. Are all 15 hashtags relevant and non-generic (no single common words)?
7. Is source_check_flags non-empty for anything that should be flagged but
   wasn't?
8. Overall: does this sound like a sharp practitioner's post, or like an AI
   newsletter? Be honest.

Output:
{
  "verdict": "approve" | "revise",
  "issues": ["specific issue and where it occurs", "..."],
  "revision_instructions": "if verdict is revise, a precise, actionable
                             instruction set for the rewrite — empty string
                             if approved"
}

If verdict is "revise", the calling script will send your issues and
revision_instructions back to the Daily Carousel Generator for exactly one
rewrite pass. If it still fails after that rewrite, the run should stop and
alert a human rather than publish.
```

### 5.5 Caption & Hashtag Refinement Prompt (standalone use)

Useful if you ever repurpose a carousel's copy for a single-image post or want to regenerate just the caption without touching the slides.

```
Using the system persona above, write a fresh Instagram caption and hashtag
set for this existing carousel content:

SLIDES (headline + subtext per slide): [insert]

Requirements:
- 3-5 sentences, sharpest line first (Instagram truncates after ~125
  characters before "more")
- One specific, non-generic CTA tied to the topic
- 15 hashtags: 5 broad, 8 niche, 2 branded — no generic single-word tags
- No banned phrases or patterns from the persona's BANNED list

Output strict JSON: {"caption": "...", "hashtags": {"broad": [...],
"niche": [...], "branded": [...]}}
```

### 5.6 Slide Design Brief Prompt (Track A / SaaS template use)

Only needed if you're rendering through a template API (Bannerbear/Placid/Canva Autofill) rather than your own HTML/CSS, and want the model to also suggest which stock icon or layout variant fits each slide.

```
For each slide in this carousel JSON: [insert slides array from 5.3]

Suggest, per slide:
- "icon_search_term": a 2-3 word search term for a relevant icon in a stock
  icon library (e.g. "brain circuit", "upward arrow chart") — skip if the
  slide is text-only by design
- "layout_variant": one of ["text-only", "text-with-icon", "text-with-number",
  "text-with-quote-mark"] — pick based on what best supports that slide's
  content, not the same variant for every slide

Output strict JSON: {"slides": [{"slide_number": 1, "icon_search_term": "...",
"layout_variant": "..."}, ...]}
```

### 5.7 Trend-Reaction Mega-Prompt (optional, same-day newsjacking)

Use this in place of the day's planned topic when something genuinely relevant broke in AI/tech/marketing that morning — swap it in manually, or feed it real search/news results if you wire up a news API step.

```
Something notable just happened in AI/tech/marketing today:

EVENT SUMMARY: [insert a factual summary of the actual news item, from a
verified source]
SOURCE: [insert source name/link]

Using the system persona above, write today's carousel reacting to this,
following the exact structure and JSON schema from the Daily Carousel

```

## 6. Compliance, Risk & Platform Policy

- **Official API use is compliant; automating engagement is not.** Everything built in this guide — generating content and publishing it through Meta's own Content Publishing API — is exactly what that API is for. Automating *engagement* to force growth (auto-follow/unfollow loops, mass auto-liking, comment-pod bots, automated mass-DMs) is a violation of Instagram's Terms of Use and Community Guidelines and risks action blocks or a permanent ban on the account you're trying to grow. This guide deliberately does not include that — growth here comes from consistent, genuinely useful posting, not simulated engagement.
- **Disclose AI-generated content.** The publish call in Section 4.5 sets `is_ai_generated: true` on the carousel container, which is Meta's own supported mechanism for this disclosure (only needs setting on the carousel container itself, not each child image). Keep it on — Meta has been moving toward requiring this, and it costs you nothing.
- **Rate limits.** At one post a day you're far under either limit Meta's docs cite (50–100 posts/24h depending on which page of their docs you're reading) — but check `GET /{ig-user-id}/content_publishing_limit` before you ever consider posting more than once a day.
- **Copyright.** Never have the model fabricate or lightly-paraphrase a specific statistic, study finding, or quote as if verified — the FACTUAL DISCIPLINE rule in Section 5.1 and the critic pass in 5.4 exist specifically to catch this. If you want to cite a real, specific stat, look it up and paste it into the prompt yourself rather than trusting the model to recall it correctly.
- **The one non-negotiable manual checkpoint.** Even with the critic pass, spend 10 minutes once a week skimming the coming week's queued JSON output before it publishes (Section 4.7's log makes this easy — just read the week's entries). This is the cheapest possible insurance against a subtle factual error or an off-brand take running unattended for a week.

## 7. Growth Playbook

Automation gets content out the door; these are the levers that actually move follower count once it's out there.

**Hook formulas that consistently outperform generic openers:**
- The specific-number claim: "I cut [specific process] from X to Y using this"
- The confident contrarian: "[popular belief] is backwards — here's why"
- The named mistake: "The prompting mistake that wastes half your [tool] outputs"
- The before/after: "What my [workflow] looked like before vs. after [change]"
- The direct callout: "If you're still doing [common practice], stop"

**What actually gets saves and shares (the signals Instagram's algorithm rewards most for reach):** content that's *useful to revisit* — frameworks, checklists, prompt structures, comparisons — saves harder than pure opinion content, which tends to win comments instead. Aim for a mix across the week rather than optimizing purely for one signal.

**Comment-bait that doesn't feel cheap:** end on a genuine, specific either/or question tied to the topic ("which of these two prompting approaches has actually worked for you?") rather than a generic "thoughts?" — specificity is what gets a real reply instead of silence.

**Repurposing:** the hook + slide 2's core insight from a strong-performing carousel is usually enough raw material for a 30-45 second talking-head Reel script, and slide 1's headline alone makes a good Story teaser pointing back at the feed post. Both are optional extensions once the core carousel pipeline is stable — resist adding them before the daily carousel loop itself is reliable.
